# Interview loop prep (candidate side)

Everything here applies to B2B and B2C alike unless a row says otherwise: the improvisation exercises, the value of quantified proof, and the resilience/coachability signal are identical in both worlds (convergence).

## The loop, stage by stage

| Stage              | Typical length | What is tested                                      | How to prepare                                                         |
| ------------------ | -------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Recruiter screen   | 15-30 min      | Basic fit, quota history, comp expectations         | One-liner versions of your top stories; a deflection-ready comp answer |
| Behavioral (STAR)  | 45-60 min      | Evidence of the competencies below                  | The story bank, rehearsed at all three lengths                         |
| Skills / role-play | 30-45 min      | Discovery, objection handling, composure, the close | Mock-call plan; rehearse aloud, not silently                           |
| Panel / final      | 60-90 min      | Strategic thinking, fit, questions you ask          | Company research; the 30-60-90 plan if requested                       |
| References         | -              | Facts, yes/no                                       | Brief each reference on the exact numbers you claimed                  |

Stage structure is from published sales-hiring guides; treat lengths as typical, not fixed.

## Prospecting/outbound

*What questions would you ask to qualify a prospect?* (Close.com)
*What's your process?* (Gong)
*What kind of sales cycles are you used to?* (Salesforce)
*How do you find new clients?* (Workable)
*How do you use social media in your sales process?* (Close.com)
*How would you approach cold calling/emailing?* (Implied from Gong)
*What's your strategy for building a prospect list?* (Inferred from experience questions)
*How do you research prospects before reaching out?* (Inferred from Gong's research emphasis)
*What tools do you use for prospecting?* (Inferred from technology questions)
*How do you prioritize which prospects to contact first?* (Pipeline management)

## Discovery

*Walk me through a successful sales process. What is your role?* (Workable)
*Tell me about a recent sale you won and your role in it.* (Salesforce)
*Walk me through one of your most strategic sales cycles, from contact to contract.* (Gong)
*What does a normal sales day look like for you?* (Salesforce)
*How do you approach discovery calls?* (Inferred from Gong's process questions)
*What questions do you ask to understand a prospect's pain points?* (Inferred from discovery focus)
*How do you uncover the real business impact of a problem?* (Value selling)
*How do you differentiate between symptoms and root causes?* (Diagnostic selling)
*What frameworks do you use for discovery (SPIN, MEDDPICC, etc.)?* (Methodology)
*How do you prepare for a discovery call?* (Preparation emphasis from Gong)

## Objection handling

*How would you deal with a dream client who is being difficult?* (Close.com)
*What's a problem you faced where you had to think outside the box to solve it?* (Salesforce)
*Give me an example of a time when you were forced to comply with a policy you didn't agree with. How did you handle it?* (Gong - leadership, but applies to objection handling)
*How do you handle price objections?* (Common sales scenario)
*How do you respond when a prospect says they need to think about it?* (Stalling objection)
*What do you do when a prospect says they're happy with their current provider?* (Incumbent objection)
*How do you handle feature/functionality objections?* (Product-specific)
*What's your process for uncovering the real objection behind a stall?* (Technique)
*How do you practice and improve your objection handling skills?* (Development focus)

## Closing/negotiation

*When do you stop pursuing a customer, and why?* (Workable)
*How have you turned around a losing streak?* (Gong - experience)
*Describe and rate the personalities of two previous coworkers and a former boss.* (Gong - relates to negotiation styles)
*What's your closing technique or approach?* (Direct question)
*How do you create urgency without being pushy?* (Negotiation skill)
*How do you handle procurement or legal negotiations?* (Complex sales)
*What's your approach to discounting and concessions?* (Pricing negotiation)
*How do you know when a prospect is ready to buy?* (Buying signals)
*What's your strategy for multi-stakeholder deals?* (Complex selling)
*How do you handle last-minute objections or delays?* (Close techniques)

## Pipeline management

*How do you measure sales goals for my position?* (Salesforce - questions to ask)
*How do you prioritize your pipeline?* (Inferred from pipeline management)
*What metrics do you track in your pipeline?* (Pipeline health)
*How often do you review and update your pipeline?* (Discipline)
*What criteria do you use to move opportunities between stages?* (Stage progression)
*How do you forecast your sales?* (Accuracy)
*What do you do with stale opportunities in your pipeline?* (Hygiene)
*How do you balance hunting vs. farming in your pipeline?* (New business vs. expansion)
*What tools do you use for pipeline management?* (Technology)
*How do you coach yourself or others on pipeline management?* (Leadership)

*Would you be willing to do a mock sales call with me right now?* (Gong - magic bullet)

## Strengtheners for your interview
- Named target accounts (B2B) or a named farming area/lead source plan (B2C).
- Specific numbers with their benchmark source.
- A stated cadence for manager check-ins.

## Weakener: generic phase labels with no numbers - it reads as a template found online.

## Brag book
A tabbed portfolio, presented when performance comes up:
- Resume.
- Sales numbers.
- Rankings.
- Awards.
- Reviews.
- Reference letters.

Near-mandatory in medical/pharma sales, differentiating everywhere else. Every number in it must have passed the evidence audit; a brag book is a verification target, not a decoration.

## Questions to ask the interviewer
Ask at least two, taking them from the top - they test transparency and generate diligence data at the same time. They are in ask-first order; the axes behind that order live in offer-diligence-and-comp.md § Diligence questions, and for B2C the draw question ranks with the first.

1. "What percentage of people in this role, on this team, hit quota last quarter?"
2. "How many reps carry what quota, against what company goal?" (Detects over-hired quota capacity.)
3. "What did the last person promoted out of this role do, and how long did it take?"
4. "What does ramp look like, and how is quota treated during it?"
5. B2C: "Walk me through the draw - recovery schedule, and what happens to a negative balance if I leave."

## Avoid:
- Questions answerable by the website.
- Pure comp questions before an offer.
- Yes/no questions.

## Weak answers that lose the room
- Feature-dumping in any role-play; pitching before a single question.
- "I like talking to people" as the motivation for sales disqualifier).
- Generic stories with no numbers, or numbers that do not cross-multiply.
- Claiming 100%+ attainment in every period - itself a red flag.
- No questions for the panel, or only comp questions.
- Passivity: never asking for the close, in the role-play or for the job itself.
